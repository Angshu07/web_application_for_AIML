01 — Introduction

What is Artificial Intelligence?

AI is the simulation of human intelligence in machines — enabling them to learn, reason, and self-correct. ML is the engine driving it forward, building models from data rather than explicit rules.

🧠

Artificial Intelligence

AI encompasses any technique enabling machines to mimic cognitive functions: visual perception, speech recognition, decision-making, and language understanding. Founded formally in 1956 at the Dartmouth Conference, AI has evolved through multiple cycles of optimism and "winters" to become today's most transformative technology. Modern AI systems can compose music, diagnose cancer, drive cars, and generate human-like text.

⚙️

Machine Learning

ML is a subset of AI where algorithms improve automatically through experience. Instead of programming explicit rules, engineers feed data to ML models which learn statistical patterns. This breakthrough — pioneered by Arthur Samuel in 1959 — shifted computing from rule-based systems to data-driven intelligence. Today's large language models, recommendation engines, and fraud-detection systems are all products of ML at scale.

🔬

Deep Learning

Deep Learning is a subfield of ML using multi-layered artificial neural networks (ANNs) loosely inspired by the human brain. With sufficient data and compute, deep networks can automatically learn hierarchical feature representations — pixels → edges → shapes → objects for images; characters → words → sentences → meaning for text. The 2012 ImageNet breakthrough by AlexNet ignited the modern deep learning era.

🌐

AI vs ML vs DL

These three terms are nested subsets: AI is the broadest field (any machine intelligence), ML is the dominant modern approach to building AI systems (learning from data), and Deep Learning is a specific class of ML using neural networks with many layers. All deep learning is ML; all ML is AI — but not all AI requires ML, and not all ML uses deep learning.

02 — Basic Concepts

Core Paradigms & Techniques

The foundational building blocks of all modern AI systems — understanding these unlocks how every application from Netflix recommendations to ChatGPT actually works.

01

Supervised Learning

The model learns a mapping function from labelled input-output pairs. A classifier trained on 10,000 labelled chest X-rays learns to flag pneumonia. Algorithms include Decision Trees, SVM, k-NN, Logistic Regression, and modern neural networks. The key challenge is obtaining high-quality labelled data at scale.

02

Unsupervised Learning

The algorithm discovers hidden structure in unlabelled data. K-Means Clustering groups customers by purchase behaviour. PCA (Principal Component Analysis) reduces dimensionality while preserving variance. Autoencoders compress and reconstruct data to learn latent representations. No teacher is needed — the algorithm finds its own signal.

03

Reinforcement Learning

An agent interacts with an environment, taking actions and receiving scalar rewards. Through millions of trial-and-error episodes, the agent learns a policy maximising cumulative reward. DeepMind's AlphaGo and AlphaZero mastered games at superhuman level this way. Applications include robotics, trading, ad bidding, and autonomous driving.

04

Neural Networks

Composed of interconnected layers of artificial neurons with learnable weights, NNs transform inputs through nonlinear activations. Backpropagation and gradient descent tune weights to minimise prediction error. Modern architectures — CNNs for images, Transformers for language — have proven universally capable function approximators given sufficient scale.

05

Transformers & Attention

Introduced in 2017's "Attention Is All You Need" paper, Transformers process sequences by computing pairwise attention weights between every token. This allows parallel processing and long-range dependency capture. GPT-4, Claude, Gemini, and virtually every modern LLM is a Transformer. The architecture also dominates computer vision (ViTs) and protein folding (AlphaFold).

06

Overfitting & Generalisation

A model that memorises training data but performs poorly on new data has overfit. Regularisation techniques (Dropout, L1/L2 penalties, early stopping), cross-validation, data augmentation, and larger datasets combat overfitting. The bias-variance tradeoff governs the fundamental tension between model complexity and generalisation — the central challenge of ML practice.

05 — Ethics

Ethical Concerns & Challenges

The power that makes AI transformative also makes its misuse dangerous. These concerns are not theoretical — they manifest in real harms today and demand urgent, sustained attention.

⚖️ Bias & Discrimination

AI systems trained on biased historical data learn to reproduce and amplify existing inequalities. COMPAS recidivism algorithms showed higher false-positive rates for Black defendants. Amazon's AI hiring tool downgraded resumes mentioning "women's". Facial recognition error rates are 34% higher for darker-skinned women than lighter-skinned men. Bias auditing, diverse training data, and fairness constraints are necessary — but not yet standard — practice.

🔐 Privacy & Surveillance

AI systems require vast data — often personal, sensitive, and collected without meaningful consent. Facial recognition deployed in public spaces enables mass surveillance. Social media recommendation algorithms build psychological profiles used to manipulate behaviour. China's Social Credit System exemplifies the authoritarian extreme. GDPR, AI Act, and emerging national AI laws attempt to create guardrails, but enforcement lags capability.

🤷 Accountability Gap

When an AI system harms someone — a denied loan, a misdiagnosis, a wrongful arrest — existing legal frameworks struggle to assign responsibility. Is it the model? The data? The deploying company? The developer? The "black box" opacity of deep neural networks makes auditing decisions nearly impossible, creating a dangerous accountability vacuum that regulators are scrambling to address through explainability requirements.

💼 Labour Disruption

IMF estimates that AI could affect 40% of jobs globally, with advanced economies more exposed. While AI historically creates new job categories, the transition speed exceeds retraining capacity. Routine cognitive tasks — data entry, basic legal research, customer support — face near-term displacement. Without coordinated policy responses including universal reskilling programmes and updated social safety nets, the disruption risk is acute.

🎭 Deepfakes & Misinformation

Generative AI can synthesise photorealistic video and audio of any person saying or doing anything. Political deepfakes already exist in multiple countries. AI-generated disinformation at scale threatens democratic processes, criminal trials, and social trust. Watermarking standards, provenance tracking (C2PA), and platform detection tools are emerging but face a fundamental asymmetry: generation is cheaper than detection.

⚔️ Autonomous Weapons

Lethal autonomous weapons systems (LAWS) that select and engage targets without human involvement are under development by multiple state actors. The UN has debated but not achieved a ban. The core concern: removing meaningful human control from life-and-death decisions violates fundamental principles of international humanitarian law. AI-enabled cyberwarfare and infrastructure attacks present equally serious risks to global security.

✦ BONUS TOPIC — 06

🛡️ AI Safety & Value Alignment

As AI systems grow more capable, ensuring they reliably pursue goals aligned with human values becomes one of the most consequential research challenges in history. AI Alignment is the field studying how to ensure advanced AI systems do what their operators and humanity actually want — even as they become more capable, autonomous, and general than their creators anticipated.

Leading researchers at Anthropic, OpenAI, DeepMind, and academic institutions have identified a fundamental risk: a sufficiently capable AI optimising for a misspecified objective could cause catastrophic harm — not out of malice, but through indifference to values that were never properly encoded. This is the alignment problem, and it becomes more dangerous as capabilities increase.

🔍 Interpretability

Mechanistic interpretability research tries to understand what computations occur inside neural networks — reverse-engineering "circuits" that implement specific behaviours. Anthropic's research on superposition and feature geometry is gradually mapping how concepts are stored and retrieved in large models, building toward auditable AI systems.

📜 Constitutional AI

Anthropic's Constitutional AI approach trains models to follow a set of principled guidelines using self-critique and revision. Rather than relying solely on human feedback for every edge case, the model learns to evaluate and improve its own responses against constitutional principles — scaling oversight beyond what direct human annotation enables.

🔒 Robustness

Robust AI systems behave safely even under distributional shift — when deployed in conditions that differ from training. Adversarial examples, prompt injections, and unexpected edge cases all test robustness. Red-teaming, adversarial training, and formal verification are among the techniques used to harden systems against failure modes.

👁️ Scalable Oversight

As AI capabilities approach and exceed human expert level in specific domains, humans lose the ability to directly verify AI outputs. Scalable oversight research — including debate, recursive reward modelling, and AI-assisted evaluation — develops methods for maintaining meaningful human control even when AI surpasses human performance on the evaluated task.

🔬 The stakes: Getting AI safety right is the most important engineering and governance challenge of the next decade. The decisions made now — about training methods, deployment constraints, and regulatory frameworks — will shape AI's trajectory for generations. This is not science fiction; it is urgent applied research.

✦ BONUS TOPIC — 07

🚀 Future Frontiers of AI

Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) — a system capable of performing any intellectual task a human can, with genuine flexibility, common sense, and reasoning — represents the long-term horizon of AI research. Whether AGI arrives in years, decades, or centuries — or is even possible — remains one of the most debated questions in science and philosophy. Most researchers believe systems significantly more capable than today's exist are plausible by 2035–2050.

In the near term (2–5 years), expect transformative progress across multiple fronts: Multimodal AI that seamlessly integrates vision, speech, text, and action; Agentic AI systems that plan and execute complex multi-step tasks autonomously over hours or days; and AI-Robotics convergence enabling systems that can manipulate the physical world with human-like dexterity.

⚛️ Quantum ML

Quantum computers can represent and process exponentially more states than classical hardware. Quantum ML algorithms promise speedups for optimisation, sampling, and linear algebra — potentially unlocking training scales and inference speeds beyond what classical hardware can achieve. IBM, Google, and startups like IonQ are building toward the threshold of quantum advantage in ML workloads.

🌍 AI Democratisation

Open-source models (Llama, Mistral, Gemma) running on consumer hardware, edge AI on smartphones, and foundation model APIs accessible to developers worldwide are distributing AI capabilities far beyond today's centralised incumbents. This democratisation could be the most important driver of global human development in the coming decade — putting expert-level tools in the hands of a billion people who currently lack access to them.

🧬 AI & Science

AI is beginning to serve as a co-scientist — formulating hypotheses, designing experiments, and synthesising literature at superhuman speed. AI-accelerated materials science could yield room-temperature superconductors, better batteries, and novel catalysts. AI-driven climate models are improving weather prediction and carbon capture research. The convergence of AI with synthetic biology could revolutionise medicine and food production.

🧠 Neuromorphic & Bio-Inspired AI

Current deep learning is computationally expensive and energy-intensive — GPT-4 training consumed an estimated 50 GWh. Neuromorphic chips (Intel's Loihi, IBM's NorthPole) mimic the brain's sparse, event-driven computation, achieving orders-of-magnitude better energy efficiency. Spiking neural networks, dendritic computing, and in-memory processing represent the next frontier of efficient AI hardware.
